Ek Kitaab

a small book, for the one who made me want to become someone worth loving

— for Ainain

for Ainain, always.

Main maafi maangta hoon — not with flowers, not with silence,
but with a promise carved plainer than any shayari:
I will never scream at you again.
I will never make you scream.
I will not drag yesterday into today's light,
I will not hold the past like a knife behind my back.

Ghalib once wrote that even wounds, if given by the right hand, are welcomed —
but I don't want to be a wound to you. Not once more.
I want to be the hand that steadies, not the storm that shakes.

تمہیں تکلیف دینا، خود کو سزا دینا تھا —
اب نہ وہ غصہ، نہ وہ گزرا ہوا کل۔
بس ایک وعدہ — صرف محبت، اور سکون۔

(Hurting you was punishing myself —
now, no more anger, no more yesterday.
Just one promise: only love, and peace.)

This is page one, so that every page after this stands on solid ground.

1

Rumi said the lover is not one who seeks beauty,
but one who has been chosen by it, helplessly.
That is what happened to me.

عینین — تم صرف محبت نہیں ہو، تم میری دعا ہو،
وہ دعا جو میں نے کبھی مانگی بھی نہیں تھی،
پھر بھی مل گئی، جیسے خدا نے خود فیصلہ کیا ہو۔

(Ainain — you are not just love, you are my prayer,
the prayer I never even asked for,
yet it arrived anyway, as if God decided it Himself.)

You are the sentence I rewrite in my head before I sleep.
You are the reason "home" stopped being a place
and became a person instead.

2

Faiz wrote of loving someone the way one loves a homeland —
fiercely, patiently, forever, even from far away.
I love you like that. Like a man loves the one country
he'd never trade for any other.

محبت میں نہ کوئی حساب ہوتا ہے، نہ کوئی شرط —
تم سے محبت کرنا، میری سانس لینے جیسا ہے۔
بنا سوچے، بنا رکے، ہمیشہ۔

(In love, there's no accounting, no condition —
loving you is like breathing.
Unthought, unpaused, forever.)

I will love you to death, Ainain.
Not as a figure of speech — as a fact I intend to prove,
one ordinary Tuesday, one bad mood survived, one hand held
at a time, for as long as I am given days.

3

Dostoevsky said hell is the suffering of being unable to love.
You saved me from that hell before I even knew I was in it.

تمہارے بعد کچھ اور چاہنے کی گنجائش ہی نہیں بچی —
تم میری پہلی دعا ہو، اور میری آخری بھی۔

(After you, there's no room left to want anything else —
you are my first prayer, and my last one too.)

Kafka wrote that love is like a bridge built out of longing,
stone by stone, one letter at a time.
I am still building mine. I will never stop.

4

Not "until," not "as long as it's easy" —
forever, the plain, unfashionable, old-world word.
The word Ghalib used when he said even paradise
means nothing to him without her in it.

عینین، تم میری کتاب کا ہر صفحہ ہو،
اور میں اس کتاب کو کبھی ختم نہیں کرنا چاہتا۔

(Ainain, you are every page of my book,
and I never want this book to end.)

5

My vocabulary was never enough for this, Ainain.
I don't have Rumi's grace or Ghalib's precision or Faiz's courage —
I only have this: I am not going anywhere.

No matter the fight, no matter the silence, no matter the distance —
I will never leave you. Not today, not on the hard days,
not ever.

تمہیں چھوڑنا، خود کو چھوڑنے جیسا ہوگا —
اور میں اپنے آپ کو کبھی نہیں چھوڑوں گا۔

(Leaving you would be like leaving myself —
and I will never leave myself.)

Every promise I have ever made you, I intend to keep.
No screaming. No past. No leaving. Only this —
you, me, and every page still unwritten.

— always yours.
